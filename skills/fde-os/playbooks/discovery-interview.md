# Discovery Interview Playbook

## Objective

Understand the business objective, real workflow, actors, systems, decisions, exceptions, data, risks, baseline, and adoption context before proposing a solution.

## Preparation

1. Review available documents and prior interviews.
2. Record what is known, assumed, disputed, and missing.
3. Tailor questions to the stakeholder's role.
4. Request permission before collecting sensitive examples or recordings.
5. Prepare a project glossary and evidence log.

## Sponsor interview

Cover:

- business objective and strategic importance
- workflow scope and boundaries
- customer or beneficiary
- current pain and urgency
- baseline metrics and expected outcomes
- budget, timeline, and decision authority
- legal, regulatory, security, and reputation constraints
- project sponsor's incentives and concerns
- conditions that would stop the project

## Workflow-owner interview

Cover:

- trigger and final output
- volume, frequency, seasonality, and service levels
- participants, roles, handoffs, and approvals
- systems, spreadsheets, communication channels, and vendors
- normal path and alternative paths
- decisions, rules, tacit knowledge, and escalation
- errors, rework, incidents, and workarounds
- data quality and missing information
- how performance is measured today

## Workflow-user interview

Ask the participant to walk through a recent real case. Use prompts such as:

- Show me the last time you completed this task.
- What do you check first?
- What makes a case easy or difficult?
- What happens when information is missing?
- Which steps are not written in the SOP?
- What do new employees usually get wrong?
- Which decisions require experience?
- Who do you contact when something is unusual?
- Which tool or spreadsheet do you distrust?
- What would make you reject an AI recommendation?

## Technical interview

Cover:

- application and database inventory
- APIs, authentication, and rate limits
- identity, roles, permissions, and secrets
- data models and integration dependencies
- logs, monitoring, incidents, and support ownership
- deployment environments and change procedures
- retention, backup, recovery, and data residency
- known technical debt and planned migrations

## Closing

1. Summarize what was heard.
2. Identify disagreements and evidence gaps.
3. Confirm follow-up artifacts and owners.
4. Ask for representative normal, exceptional, and failed cases.
5. Explain the next lifecycle phase.

## Outputs

- interview notes with evidence references
- stakeholder-specific findings
- questions requiring verification
- updated glossary
- assumptions and risks
- requested evidence list

## Quality checks

- Did the interview capture real examples rather than opinions alone?
- Were exceptions explored?
- Were business incentives and adoption risks captured?
- Were statements labeled as evidence, interpretation, or assumption?
- Was sensitive information handled appropriately?
